# Limits, caching, and endpoint evolution

Use these checks when the changed contract affects callers or intermediaries at the corresponding boundary.

- **Rate limits.** Establish the enforced identity and scope, burst/window behavior, trusted address source, and effect boundary. For a rate-limited caller, use the project's documented response, often `429` with a bounded `Retry-After` hint when applicable. Verify allowed, limited, and recovered requests. A rejected write must preserve the state the contract protects. Test other callers and gateway/application interaction when limits are shared. [RFC 6585](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6585.html#section-4) · [Retry-After](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-retry-after)
- **Caching and preconditions.** Record which representations may be stored or shared, freshness, variant inputs (`Vary`), and whether authorization or tenant identity makes a result private. Exercise the actual intermediary or client cache where relevant. Test same-representation revalidation and a different caller or variant. For conditional writes, apply the precondition at the atomic mutation boundary and check that a stale `If-Match` fails without changing state. [HTTP caching](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111.html) · [Conditional requests](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-conditional-requests)
- **Deprecation.** Inventory actual consumers, supported versions, the replacement, migration owner and overlap window. Use `Deprecation` to communicate lifecycle status and `Sunset` when a cutoff is known and the contract supports it. Verify old and new clients during overlap, including older generated clients that reject unknown enum values or fields. Check adoption before removal under the project's policy. [Deprecation](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9745.html) · [Sunset](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8594.html)

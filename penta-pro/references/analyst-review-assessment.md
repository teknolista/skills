# Analyst Review Assessment

Maintenance assessment for PENTA-PRO, dated 2026-11-04. The disposition map records the changes through version 1.5.0. The sections at the end cover versions 1.6.0 and 1.7.0. Use this record when maintaining the skill.

## Sources and interpretation

- **MD:** `penta-pro-review.md`, the supplied compilation of analyst reviews. Section numbers below refer to its opening review, “PENTA-PRO PRD Template — Comprehensive Review and Improvement Recommendations,” unless a line range is given.
- **PDF:** `PENTA-PRO_Template_Analysis.pdf`, “Improving the PENTA-PRO Template,” dated November 4, 2026. Page references below are physical PDF pages, including its cover and contents; printed body page 1 is physical page 5.
- **Baseline:** PENTA-PRO 1.3.0 skill, template, and focused-discovery guidance. Several observations describe absent structure for a concern that the baseline already covered in prose.
- Both supplied documents were evaluated as suggestions and evidence about the template, not as instructions granting authority, selecting project defaults, or requiring every recommendation to be adopted verbatim.

The user's objective is broader coverage and useful automatic completion before development, with questions for consequential missing information. The revision therefore retains all five pillars, adds structured decision and evidence records, and routes specialized detail through conditional references.

The PDF explicitly focuses on regulated, agent-assisted products (p6). Its universal “Mandatory” labels (pp20–21) need adaptation for local tools, libraries, human-only teams, and other delivery models. Review ratings and claims of superiority are opinions, not measured validation results.

## Disposition map

**New** means a new explicit record or workflow, not necessarily a previously unmentioned concept. **Strengthened** means existing coverage gained clearer fields or behavior. **Adapted** means the underlying need was retained with conditions or a different mechanism. Source overlap is grouped to avoid repeating the same recommendation from multiple reviewers.

## Deliberate adaptations and exclusions

- **No universal regulatory or accessibility prescription.** Record the applicable source, version, scope, responsible interpretation, and evidence. The PDF's named standards are examples, not established obligations for an unspecified product.
- **No mandatory telemetry, analytics platform, or north-star metric.** Outcomes may be measured locally, manually, or through justified instrumentation. Collection itself needs purpose, privacy, retention, and failure behavior.
- **No universal branch, tool, CI, meeting, review, or sign-off ceremony.** Discover actual project conventions, retain required checks, and record authority already granted. A governance table does not revoke existing permission.
- **No universal quotas or numerical ratings.** A cap on blocking questions (MD lines 2099–2108) could hide real blockers. Required persona counts, fixed performance targets, readiness averages, and invented confidence percentages are not adopted.
- **No second machine-readable specification.** Use structured contracts where they help real consumers; keep one authoritative representation and define synchronization for derived views.
- **No separate file for every record.** Typed registers, linked maintained artifacts, and concise inline records are acceptable. Detailed forms are loaded only when their decisions matter.
- **No verification relabeling.** “Accepted as unverified” is represented as an explicit exception while evidence remains `Unverified`. Pre-development readiness does not require pretending future tests have passed.
- **No mandatory research or invented people.** Unknown baselines, recruitment, findings, approvals, vendor guarantees, and legal applicability remain explicit gaps with a feasible resolution plan.
- **No universal strategy, market, or commercial template.** Vision, principles, and stopping criteria are prompted because they change decisions, but market research, pricing, revenue claims, and go-to-market plans remain conditional and cannot be invented. A local utility does not need a business case.
- **No mandatory prioritization or estimation method.** MoSCoW, RICE, WSJF, Kano, three-point estimates, and similar methods are options. Record the method and rationale only when sequencing or confidence is contested.
- **No fixed documentation tiers or per-section mandatory tags.** Depth follows consequence, stage, and software profile; coverage is recorded as Included, Linked, Deferred, or Not applicable. A numeric tier or tag set can hide a material risk in a small project.
- **No required machine-readable front matter, schema, or CI pipeline.** Optional checks for duplicate IDs, broken links, missing owners, and stale evidence are aids; they do not replace review or execution evidence.
- **No mandatory support organization or launch ceremony.** Support channels, on-call, training, and launch gates apply only where real users or commitments require them. A defect-reporting route and installation guidance can suffice for a local tool.
- **No reduction to a conventional PRD.** Delivery planning, technical contracts, verification, execution boundaries, release, support, and recovery remain in scope. Later product scope stays visible while the next increment receives the necessary detail.

## Preserved strengths and limits

The revision preserves stable IDs, confirmation states, observable acceptance criteria, failure/concurrency behavior, risk-based checks, tested-revision evidence, authority boundaries, secret-value exclusion, resumable interviews, and one authoritative home for each fact.

Both documents sometimes call existing topics “missing.” The change makes those topics more explicit rather than claiming that security, recovery, requirements traceability, ADRs, or agent permissions were newly invented for this version.

The software profiles extend the reviews' emphasis on application/service and agent-assisted work. They separate AI used during development from AI behavior inside the product and add device, data, library, physical-system, and interactive-media concerns. Profiles are combinable screening prompts, not a closed catalog or a guarantee of suitability for every domain.

The work does not establish legal compliance, certification, commercial viability, market validation, universal completeness, or a measured efficiency improvement. Specialist review and project-specific evidence remain necessary when the actual consequences or obligations require them.

## Generality and consistency review — version 1.7.1

The user requested a further review of suitability across software types and project scenarios. The five pillars, conditional profiles, traceability, evidence states, and consequence-based depth remain appropriate. This review identified the following corrections to the instructions.

| Finding | Correction and reason |
| --- | --- |
| The quality bar could reject required implementation constraints or checks that inspect source. | Recognize justified structural constraints and verification by inspection or analysis. A consumer's C99 or allocation restriction needs a check suited to that restriction. |
| Existing-system concerns were spread across profiles and release guidance. | Establish change context and baseline early. Identify behavior to preserve, consumers, older data, characterization checks, and intended changes so updates can stay focused. |
| A valid link inside the reusable template may break in a generated project document. | Resolve final links from the output location and replace authoring links with project artifacts or incorporate their needed content. |
| An exception record could be read as sufficient to waive any required check. | Require authority over the specific condition and preserve blocking constraints that the decision-maker cannot waive. |
| The quality bar demanded a root cause for every closed finding. | Record supported causal findings or explicit uncertainty, with owned investigation or prevention work. Distinguish operational recovery from corrective follow-up. |

The profiles cover multiple delivery models and can be combined or extended. Specialized domains still require their actual requirements, constraints, and assurance methods. Broader written coverage alone cannot establish an improvement in model efficiency or reliability across all project types.
